Picture this. You walk into a room and everyone is talking about apples.

"Apples changed my life."

"I eat apples every day."

"You are falling behind if you do not own apples."

Everyone is loud. Everyone is excited. So you try the apples you are handed, and they taste bad. Bitter. Rotten.

Now you start to doubt yourself. Is something wrong with me? Am I bad at tasting? Should I take a course on apples?

Here is what nobody tells you. The apples in that room are rotten. And people are still acting like they are the best apples in the world.

## This is what AI feels like right now

If you work in design, you have probably felt this. You try the tools. Sometimes you get something useful. Often you get something that looks right and is quietly wrong. Meanwhile your feed is full of people saying it changed everything.

Some of the rotten apples you have been handed:

- **"AI will replace your job."** Fear, mostly used to sell something.
- **"Learn the perfect prompt."** A trick that goes stale every few months.
- **"This new model is 500x better."** Marketing.
- **"You need AI or you are finished."** Urgency made up by people selling courses.
- **"AI is about to become conscious."** Either fear or fantasy.

None of these help you do better work on Monday.

## Two ways people get stuck

Most designers land in one of two camps.

The first camp thinks it is already over. They panic, buy courses and collect prompt templates.

The second camp shares a screenshot of AI failing at simple maths, laughs, and goes back to work feeling safe.

Both camps start from a belief, not a test. One gives up before trying. The other dismisses it without understanding it. Neither is a good way to make career decisions.

## A third way

There is a better option, and it is the one designers already know: understand how the thing works, then decide how to use it.

You would never judge a new product by the noise around it. You would open it up, watch people use it, and see where it breaks. AI deserves the same treatment.

When you understand what is happening under the hood, a few things change:

- Strange results stop being a mystery. You can see why they happened.
- You know when to trust an answer and when to check it.
- You know what to give the tool to get useful work back.
- You can say clearly which parts of your job it can help with, and which parts stay yours.

## The green apple

Real AI, the useful kind, is a green apple that most people have not been handed yet. It is not magic and it is not a threat. It is a tool with clear strengths and clear limits.
