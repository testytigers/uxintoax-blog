    a: "Usability testing is a type of user testing focused on how easy it is to complete tasks. User testing is broader — it can include preference testing, concept testing, or market research. All usability tests are user tests, but not all user tests are usability tests."

## The Short Answer

Usability testing is watching real people try to use your product — and paying attention to where they struggle.

It's the single most valuable thing you can do as a UX designer. One testing session will reveal more than a thousand opinions.

---

## The 5 Key Steps

### 1. Define Objectives
What do you need to learn? "Can users check out?" is a goal. "Why do users abandon the cart?" is a research question.

### 2. Choose Participants
5-8 users who match your target audience. Real users > colleagues > friends.

### 3. Create Tasks
Give realistic scenarios, not instructions:
- ❌ "Click the blue button, then fill in your email"
- ✅ "You want to buy a gift for a friend. Show us how you'd complete the purchase."

### 4. Run the Test
- Let users think out loud
- Don't help them
- Watch, don't lead
- Record sessions for review

### 5. Analyze and Act
- Identify patterns across sessions
- Prioritize problems by severity
- Create actionable recommendations

## Moderated vs Unmoderated

| Moderated | Unmoderated |
|-----------|-------------|
| Live researcher guides session | Users complete tasks alone online |
| Can ask follow-up questions | Cheaper, faster |
| Better for complex tasks | More participants possible |
| Tools: Zoom, Lookback | Tools: Maze, UserTesting |

---

## What to Measure

**Success Rate** — Did they complete the task? (Yes/No)

**Time on Task** — How long did it take? (vs. target benchmark)

**Error Rate** — How many mistakes did they make?

**SUS Score** — System Usability Scale (10 questions, 0-100 score)

**Task Satisfaction** — "How easy was this?" (1-5 scale)

## Common Mistakes

- **Leading users** — Don't suggest solutions
- **Testing too early** — Test wireframes, not just polished designs
- **One user at a time** — Watch others observe and learn
- **Not recruiting real users** — Colleagues know too much
- **Ignoring the why** — Note where they struggle, then ask why

---

## The Bottom Line

Usability testing is the fastest path to better design. Five users. Real tasks. No help. You'll learn more in one session than in a month of opinions. Start with a prototype and 5 users. It changes everything.
