## The Short Answer

A great UX portfolio shows **how you think**, not just what you designed.

Recruiters spend 30 seconds looking at your portfolio. Make those 30 seconds count.

---

## The 5 Elements of Every Case Study

### 1. The Problem (1 paragraph)
What were you solving? Who were you solving it for? Why did it matter?

### 2. Your Role (1-2 sentences)
What did you do? What didn't you do? Be specific.

### 3. The Process (2-3 paragraphs)
Research → Ideation → Design → Testing. Show your thinking, not just the output.

### 4. Key Decisions (2-3 examples)
"What I chose to do, and why." This is where you separate yourself from designers who just make things pretty.

### 5. The Outcome (1-2 paragraphs)
What happened? Metrics. Learnings. What you'd do differently.

---

## Project Selection

### Good Projects
- Real users, real problems
- Clear before/after
- Demonstrates research skills
- Shows iteration

### Bad Projects
- Concept projects without research
- "Redesigned Spotify" (overdone)
- Projects where you only did visual design
- Projects you can't explain in detail

---

## Portfolio Structure

**Homepage** — Who you are, what you do, 3 featured projects

**Case Studies** — 4-6 deep dives

**About** — Your background, skills, personality

**Contact** — Easy to reach you

---

## Presentation Tips

- Start with a summary, not a screenshot
- Show sketches and iterations, not just final screens
- Include real data and metrics
- Use video walkthroughs for complex flows
- Keep text concise — use headings and bullet points

---

## The Bottom Line

Your portfolio isn't a gallery. It's a story. Tell the story of how you solve problems, not just how you make things pretty.
