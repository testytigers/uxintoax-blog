## The Short Answer

User research is how you learn what users actually need — not what you think they need.

It's the difference between designing something beautiful and designing something that works.

---

## What Is User Research?

User research is the process of understanding your users through observation, interviews, and data analysis. It answers:

- Who are your users?
- What problems do they have?
- How do they currently solve these problems?
- What frustrates them?
- What would make their lives easier?

---

## Types of User Research

### Qualitative Research (The "Why")

**User Interviews**
- One-on-one conversations (30-60 minutes)
- Open-ended questions
- Best for: understanding motivations and behaviors
- Sample size: 3-8 users per segment

**Observational Research**
- Watch users interact with products
- Contextual inquiry (observe in their environment)
- Best for: understanding real behavior vs. stated behavior
- Sample size: 5-10 users

**Focus Groups**
- Group discussions (6-10 participants)
- Best for: exploring attitudes and perceptions
- Note: group dynamics can influence answers

**Diary Studies**
- Users record their experiences over days/weeks
- Best for: understanding habits and long-term behavior

### Quantitative Research (The "How Many")

**Surveys**
- Structured questions with statistical analysis
- Best for: validating patterns across large audiences
- Sample size: 50-500+ respondents

**A/B Testing**
- Compare two designs with real users
- Best for: making data-driven design decisions
- Requires: significant traffic and statistical power

**Analytics Analysis**
- Study how users actually interact with your product
- Best for: understanding behavior patterns at scale
- Tools: Google Analytics, Hotjar, Mixpanel

## The Research Plan

Every research project needs a plan:

1. **Define objectives** — What do you need to learn?
2. **Identify participants** — Who are your users?
3. **Choose methods** — Qualitative, quantitative, or both?
4. **Recruit participants** — Find 5-10 users who match
5. **Prepare materials** — Interview guides, survey questions, tasks
6. **Conduct research** — Run sessions
7. **Analyze findings** — Identify patterns and insights
8. **Share results** — Present to stakeholders
9. **Take action** — Apply insights to design

---

## Research Methods Decision Tree

**Need to understand motivations?** → User interviews
**Need to validate a pattern?** → Surveys or analytics
**Need to see actual behavior?** → Observational research
**Need to test a design?** → Usability testing
**Need to prioritize features?** → Surveys with ranking

---

## Common Research Mistakes

**❌ Leading questions**
"What do you think of this button?" → "What frustrates you about finding the checkout?"

**❌ Testing with colleagues**
Colleagues know too much about your product. Find real users.

**❌ Ignoring negative feedback**
If users hate something, that's your biggest opportunity.

**❌ Over-relying on one method**
Combine qualitative + quantitative for complete understanding.
