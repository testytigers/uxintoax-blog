## The Short Answer

Design thinking is a **human-centered approach to problem-solving** that prioritizes understanding people before building solutions.

It has 5 stages: Empathize, Define, Ideate, Prototype, Test. But here's the truth — these stages aren't sequential. They're a loop. You'll revisit them constantly as you learn.

---

## The 5 Stages of Design Thinking

### Stage 1: Empathize

Understand the people you're designing for. Not what they say — what they do, feel, and struggle with.

**Methods:**
- User interviews (1-on-1 conversations)
- Observation and shadowing
- Empathy mapping
- Journey mapping
- Diary studies

**The goal:** Build genuine understanding, not just data points.

### Stage 2: Define

Frame the problem clearly based on what you learned.

**Methods:**
- Point of view statements: "[User] needs [need] because [insight]"
- Problem statements
- How might we questions
- Affinity diagramming

**The goal:** One clear problem statement that guides everything.

### Stage 3: Ideate

Brainstorm solutions without constraints. Quantity over quality.

**Methods:**
- Crazy 8s (8 ideas in 8 minutes)
- Worst Possible Idea (reverse brainstorming)
- SCAMPER technique
- Mind mapping
- Brainwriting

**The goal:** Break out of obvious solutions. Explore wild ideas.

### Stage 4: Prototype

Build something tangible to test.

**Methods:**
- Paper prototypes
- Digital wireframes
- Interactive prototypes
- Service blueprints
- Role-playing

**The goal:** Make thinking visible. Learn cheap. Fail fast.

### Stage 5: Test

Validate with real users.

**Methods:**
- Usability testing
- A/B testing
- Feedback sessions
- Field studies

**The goal:** Learn what works, what doesn't, and what to iterate.

## Design Thinking Principles

1. **Human-centered** — Start with people, not technology
2. **Iterative** — Learn, refine, repeat
3. **Collaborative** — Diverse teams solve better problems
4. **Bias toward action** — Build to think, not just talk
5. **Show, don't tell** — Visualize ideas
6. **Radical collaboration** — Break down silos
7. **Show, don't tell** — Visualize ideas

## Real-World Example: Airbnb

Airbnb's turnaround is the most famous design thinking case study.

**Problem:** Revenue was stagnant. Hosts weren't signing up. Users weren't booking.

**Design thinking approach:**
1. **Empathize** — Founders flew to New York, stayed with hosts, took professional photos of listings
2. **Define** — "Hosts need better tools to showcase their spaces"
3. **Ideate** — Professional photography, better listing pages, trust signals
4. **Prototype** - Redesigned listing pages with professional photos
5. **Test** - Revenue doubled within weeks

**Key insight:** The problem wasn't the product. It was trust. Photos built trust.

## Common Design Thinking Mistakes

**❌ Skipping empathy to jump to solutions**
→ You'll solve the wrong problem beautifully

**❌ Treating stages as linear**
→ Design thinking is iterative. You'll loop back constantly

**❌ Ideating without constraints**
→ Unlimited ideas without focus lead to paralysis

**❌ Prototyping too late**
→ The best prototypes are paper. Test early.

**❌ Testing with colleagues instead of real users**
→ Colleagues know too much. Find your actual users.

## How to Run a Design Thinking Workshop

1. **Preparation (1 week)**
   - Define the problem
   - Invite diverse participants (5-8 people)
   - Gather materials (sticky notes, markers, paper)

2. **Session (2-3 hours)**
   - 20 min: Empathize (share insights)
   - 30 min: Define (create HMW questions)
   - 40 min: Ideate (Crazy 8s + voting)
   - 40 min: Prototype (paper sketches)
   - 30 min: Test (quick feedback)

3. **Follow-up (1 week)**
   - Document outcomes
   - Create next steps
   - Schedule testing

## The Bottom Line

Design thinking isn't a methodology you follow. It's a mindset you adopt.

It asks: "Who does this help?" before "What should we build?"

It prioritizes human needs over technology capabilities.

It values learning over guessing.

It's the most powerful framework for solving complex problems in 2025.
