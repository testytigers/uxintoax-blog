  - q: "What are the steps in the UX design process?"
    a: "The UX design process has 5 stages: Research (understand users), Design (create solutions), Prototype (build testable versions), Test (validate with users), and Iterate (improve based on feedback). Each stage feeds into the next in an iterative cycle."
  - q: "How long does the UX design process take?"
    a: "A full UX project typically takes 4-12 weeks depending on scope. Research: 1-2 weeks. Design: 1-3 weeks. Prototype: 1-2 weeks. Testing: 1 week. Iteration: 1-4 weeks. Simple projects can be done in 2-3 weeks."
  - q: "Can the UX process be non-linear?"
    a: "Yes. The UX process is iterative and non-linear. You'll often go back to earlier stages based on testing results. For example, testing might reveal you need more research, or prototyping might show a fundamental design problem. That's normal."

## The Short Answer

The UX design process is a framework, not a rulebook.

Here's how it actually works in practice, with real examples.

---

## Stage 1: Research (1-2 Weeks)

### What Happens
- User interviews
- Surveys
- Competitive analysis
- Data analysis

### Deliverables
- User personas
- Problem statement
- Research insights

### Tools
- Zoom (interviews)
- Typeform (surveys)
- Google Analytics (data)
- Miro (analysis)

---

## Stage 2: Design (1-3 Weeks)

### What Happens
- Information architecture
- User flows
- Wireframes (low-fi)
- Visual design (hi-fi)

### Deliverables
- Sitemap
- User flow diagrams
- Wireframes
- Visual designs

### Tools
- Figma (design)
- Miro (flows)
- Whimsical (wireframes)
- Sketch (alternative)

---

## Stage 3: Prototype (1-2 Weeks)

### What Happens
- Interactive prototype
- Micro-interactions
- Responsive variations
- Edge cases

### Deliverables
- Interactive prototype
- Prototype links
- Device variations

### Tools
- Figma (prototyping)
- Protopie (advanced)
- Framer (web)

---

## Stage 4: Test (1 Week)

### What Happens
- Moderated or unmoderated testing
- 5 users minimum
- Task-based scenarios
- Observation and notes

### Deliverables
- Test findings
- Issue severity ratings
- Recommendations

### Tools
- Maze (unmoderated)
- Lookback (moderated)
- Hotjar (recordings)
- Google Forms (surveys)

---

## Stage 5: Iterate (Ongoing)

### What Happens
- Prioritize issues
- Implement fixes
- Retest
- Repeat

### Deliverables
- Updated designs
- Updated prototype
- Updated documentation

---

## The Reality

The UX process isn't linear. You'll loop back. You'll skip steps. You'll do things out of order. That's fine. The process is a guide, not a constraint.

---

## The Bottom Line

The UX process exists to reduce risk. It doesn't exist to slow you down. Use it as a framework, adapt it to your context, and keep iterating.
