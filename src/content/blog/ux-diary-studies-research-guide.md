## The Short Answer

Diary studies capture real behavior over time.

Not what users say in a lab. What they actually do in the wild.

---

## Types of Diary Studies

### Prompt-Based
Researcher sends prompts at scheduled times. Participants respond.

### Self-Reported
Participants log experiences whenever they want. More flexible, less structured.

### Mixed
Best of both. Scheduled prompts + spontaneous entries.

---

## How to Run a Diary Study

### Phase 1: Planning (1 week)
- Define research goals
- Choose method (prompted vs self-reported)
- Select platform (app, form, journal)
- Recruit 5-15 participants

### Phase 2: Execution (1-4 weeks)
- Daily/weekly prompts
- Encourage rich responses
- Monitor engagement
- Send reminders

### Phase 3: Analysis (1-2 weeks)
- Look for patterns
- Identify pain points
- Map behavior over time
- Synthesize findings

---

## Diary Study Prompt Examples

### Day 1
"How did you start your day? What was the first app you opened?"

### Day 3
"Describe a moment today when you felt frustrated with a product. What happened?"

### Day 7
"Looking back at the past week, what patterns do you notice in your daily routine?"

---

## The Bottom Line

Diary studies reveal what one-off research misses: habits, routines, and evolving needs. They're long and require commitment. But the insights are invaluable.
