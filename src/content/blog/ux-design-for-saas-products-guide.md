  - q: "How do you design a SaaS dashboard?"
    a: "Start with the user's primary goal. Show the most important metrics first. Use progressive disclosure for advanced features. Include clear actions, not just data. Design for both new users (guided) and power users (efficient shortcuts)."
  - q: "What's the best way to design a pricing page?"
    a: "Clear comparison between plans. Highlight the recommended plan. Show value, not just features. Include FAQs. Use social proof. Make it easy to understand what you get for the price. A/B test pricing page layouts regularly."

## The Short Answer

SaaS UX is about making complex software feel simple.

Users pay monthly. If they don't see value quickly, they cancel.

---

## The 4 Pillars of SaaS UX

### 1. Onboarding
Get users to their first "aha" moment fast.

### 2. Feature Adoption
Help users discover and use key features.

### 3. Retention
Keep users engaged with regular value delivery.

### 4. Expansion
Upsell and cross-sell through smart UX.

---

## Designing the Dashboard

### What to Show
- Primary metrics relevant to the user's role
- Quick actions they'll take frequently
- Clear navigation to all features
- Personalized content based on usage

### What to Avoid
- Information overload
- Hidden primary actions
- Inconsistent layouts
- Slow load times

---

## SaaS UX Checklist

- [ ] Clear value proposition on signup
- [ ] Guided onboarding for new users
- [ ] Progress indicators in workflows
- [ ] Help content accessible at every step
- [ ] Keyboard shortcuts for power users
- [ ] Clear upgrade path
- [ ] Transparent pricing
- [ ] Self-service cancellation
- [ ] Regular feature updates visible
- [ ] Mobile responsive for key flows

---

## The Bottom Line

SaaS UX is a marathon, not a sprint. Focus on delivering consistent value. Make complex features accessible. Keep users engaged. Design for the long term.
