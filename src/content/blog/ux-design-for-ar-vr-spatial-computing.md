## The Short Answer

AR/VR UX is a fundamentally different discipline.

You're not designing for a screen. You're designing for a space.

---

## The 5 Principles of Spatial UX

### 1. Comfort First
Prevent motion sickness. Provide smooth transitions. Offer multiple navigation modes.

### 2. Natural Interaction
Use gestures people already know. Point, grab, pinch. No learning curve.

### 3. Spatial Awareness
Respect the physical environment. Don't block real-world objects.

### 4. Information Hierarchy
In 3D, depth = importance. Important items closer. Secondary items further.

### 5. Feedback
Haptic, visual, and audio feedback for every interaction. Users need to know they touched something.

---

## AR UX vs VR UX

### AR UX (Augmented Reality)
- Digital content on real world
- Real environment always visible
- Partial immersion
- Example: IKEA Place app

### VR UX (Virtual Reality)
- Fully digital environment
- Real world blocked
- Full immersion
- Example: Horizon Workrooms

---

## Designing for Comfort

### Motion Sickness Prevention
- Smooth camera movement (no teleport unless necessary)
- Fixed reference points
- Adjustable movement speed
- Comfort modes (vignette on turn)

### Accessibility
- Sitting/standing options
- One-handed controls
- Voice commands
- Adjustable UI size

---

## Spatial UX Checklist

- [ ] Comfortable movement options
- [ ] Natural gesture interactions
- [ ] Spatial audio feedback
- [ ] Environmental awareness
- [ ] Multiple input methods
- [ ] Motion sickness prevention
- [ ] Tested in real environments
- [ ] Accessibility options available
- [ ] Performance optimized (90fps+)
- [ ] Clear spatial hierarchy

---

## The Bottom Line

Spatial UX is the future of interface design. AR and VR are moving from novelty to utility. Start learning spatial thinking now. It's not about screens. It's about spaces.
