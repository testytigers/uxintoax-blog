## The Short Answer

Eye tracking answers one question: what do users see first?

Because what they don't see, they can't interact with.

---

## The 5 Eye Tracking Metrics

### Fixations
Steady gaze on one spot. Indicates information processing.

### Saccades
Rapid eye movement between fixations. The eye jumps, doesn't glide.

### Dwell Time
Total time spent looking at an element. Longer = more attention (or confusion).

### First Fixation
The very first thing users look at. Critical for hero sections and CTAs.

### Heatmap
Aggregated gaze data across multiple users. Hot = cold.

---

## How Eye Tracking Works

### Hardware
Specialized cameras track pupil position at 60-120 Hz.

### Calibration
Users look at 9 points on screen. System maps gaze to screen coordinates.

### Recording
Eye position recorded alongside screen content.

### Analysis
Data overlaid on screen: heatmaps, gaze plots, heatzones.

---

## What Eye Tracking Reveals

### F-Pattern
Users scan long pages in an F-shape. Top, left, then down.

### Z-Pattern
Users scan simple pages in a Z-shape. Top-left to bottom-right.

### Blind Spots
Users consistently miss elements in expected positions.

### Banner Blindness
Users ignore anything that looks like an ad.

---

## The Bottom Line

Eye tracking is powerful but expensive. For most teams, heatmap tools + usability testing + eye tracking theory = enough insights. Don't skip the research, but don't overspend either.
