## The Short Answer

User onboarding is the difference between someone who stays and someone who leaves.

The best onboarding doesn't feel like onboarding. It feels like the product just... works.

---

## The 3 Types of Onboarding

### 1. Pre-Onboarding
Value proposition before signup. Clear messaging. Social proof.

### 2. First-Time Onboarding
The first 60 seconds after signup. Show the 'aha moment.'

### 3. Progressive Onboarding
Guide features as users encounter them, not all at once.

---

## How to Design Each Type

### First-Time Onboarding
- **Skip option** — Let experienced users skip
- **Immediate value** — Show the 'aha' moment fast
- **Interactive** — Let them try, not just watch
- **Progress indicator** — Show how far they are
- **One primary action** — Don't overwhelm

### Progressive Onboarding
- **Contextual tooltips** — Guide when relevant
- **Empty states** — Show what's possible before content exists
- **Feature discovery** — Highlight features as they become relevant
- **Progressive disclosure** — Reveal complexity gradually

## Real Examples

### Slack
- Asks about your role
- Suggests connecting to tools you use
- Shows you a channel with activity
- Goal: Show the product in context

### Notion
- Lets you explore an empty workspace
- Shows templates when you're stuck
- Guides only when you need help
- Goal: Freedom first, guidance second

### Duolingo
- Asks about your goals
- Starts a lesson immediately
- Shows progress from day one
- Goal: Immediate engagement

---

## The Onboarding Checklist

- [ ] Clear value proposition before signup
- [ ] Skip button on all onboarding steps
- [ ] One primary action per screen
- [ ] Progress indicator visible
- [ ] Interactive, not just passive
- [ ] Empty states show what's possible
- [ ] Contextual help, not forced tutorials
- [ ] Immediate value within 60 seconds
- [ ] Follow-up emails/nudges for inactive users
- [ ] Analytics tracking at every step

---

## The Bottom Line

Great onboarding doesn't feel like onboarding. It feels like the product understands you. Design for the user's goal, not your feature list. Show value fast. Guide only when needed.
