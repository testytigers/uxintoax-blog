## The Short Answer

Dark patterns work short-term. They fail long-term.

Tricking users into subscriptions doesn't make loyal customers.

---

## Common Dark Patterns

### 1. Confirm-Shaming
"Don't be shy" — press a button and admit you don't want to subscribe.

### 2. Hidden Costs
Show price without shipping. Reveal costs at checkout.

### 3. Forced Continuity
Free trial auto-renews with no clear cancellation.

### 4. Misleading Notifications
"Someone in New York just bought this!" (when it's fake)

### 5. Roach Motions
Easy to sign up, impossible to cancel.

---

## Ethical Alternatives

| Dark Pattern | Ethical Alternative |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Hidden costs | Show all costs upfront |
| Confirm-shaming | Respectful opt-out |
| Forced continuity | Clear trial end date |
| Fake scarcity | Real availability |
| Hard cancellation | One-click unsubscribe |

---

## The Dark Patterns Checklist

- [ ] No hidden costs
- [ ] Easy cancellation paths
- [ ] Honest availability
- [ ] Respectful language
- [ ] Clear consent
- [ ] No misleading claims
- [ ] No forced continuity
- [ ] User in control

---

## The Bottom Line

Ethical design isn't just morally right — it's better for business. Trust compounds. Deception doesn't. Design honestly, and your users will stay.
